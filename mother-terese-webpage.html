Mother Teresa

Mother Teresa was a global humanitarian and Catholic nun who dedicated her life to serving the poor, the sick, and the unwanted.Born Agnes Gonxha Bojaxhiu in Skopje, Macedonia, on August 26, 1910, she moved to India as a young woman and spent years teaching in Kolkata (Calcutta). Moved by the extreme poverty and suffering she witnessed outside her convent, she left to live among the destitute and founded the Missionaries of Charity in 1950. Her religious order built hospices, schools, orphanages, and care centers for people suffering from terminal illnesses like leprosy, tuberculosis, and HIV/AIDS. For her tireless and compassionate service to humanity, she received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1979. She passed away in 1997 and was later canonized as a saint by the Catholic Church in 2016.

Early Life and Education

Mother Teresa was born Agnes Gonxha Bojaxhiu on August 26, 1910, in Skopje, Macedonia, into a devout Catholic Albanian family. Growing up with a deep sense of compassion and religious faith nurtured by her mother, she felt a strong desire to become a missionary by age twelve. In 1928, at the age of eighteen, she left home to join the Sisters of Loreto in Ireland to begin her religious training. Shortly after, she traveled to India, arriving in Calcutta in 1929. She completed her novitiate in Darjeeling—where she learned Bengali and Hindi—took her first vows in 1931, and later made her final solemn profession in 1937, adopting the title "Mother". She spent nearly two decades teaching geography and history at St. Mary's High School in Calcutta, eventually serving as the school's principal before feeling a profound inner calling to leave the convent and live among the poorest of the poor.

Teaching Career

Mother Teresa began her teaching career in Kolkata (Calcutta), India, after arriving as a young Loreto sister in 1929. For nearly two decades, she taught subjects like geography and history at St. Mary’s High School, a school attached to the Loreto convent in Entally. She learned local languages including Bengali and Hindi, took her final vows, and eventually became the school's headmistress (principal) in 1944. While she deeply enjoyed shaping young minds, witnessing the crushing poverty and suffering in the nearby slums—particularly during the 1943 Bengal famine—inspired a profound shift in her calling. In 1946, she received what she described as a "call within a call" to leave the convent school and live among the poorest of the poor, leading her to establish an open-air school and eventually found the Missionaries of Charity.

Humanitarian Work

Mother Teresa's humanitarian work was characterized by her unwavering dedication to serving the poorest and most marginalized individuals. In 1950, she founded the Missionaries of Charity, a religious congregation devoted to helping those in need. The organization established hospices, orphanages, schools, and care centers for people suffering from terminal illnesses such as leprosy, tuberculosis, and HIV/AIDS. Mother Teresa's approach emphasized compassion, dignity, and love for every individual, regardless of their social status or background. Her tireless efforts earned her international recognition, including the Nobel Peace Prize in 1979. She continued her humanitarian work until her death in 1997, leaving behind a legacy of selfless service and inspiring countless others to follow in her footsteps.
